# FinCore AI Concept:

FinCore AI, made by FinTech company Fortress Finance, is an Agentic AI that goes beyond passive content generation or simple automation; it involves autonomous software "agents" that can plan, reason, use tools, and make multi-step decisions to achieve specific financial goals. Its functions are:

  + Know Your Customer (KYC) & Onboarding: It can automatically extract data from documents, identify missing information, and generate and analyze Source of Wealth (SoW) narratives, reducing manual review time by up to 99%.
  + Anti-Money Laundering (AML): It autonomously analyze transaction patterns across multiple accounts and regions to detect anomalies and trigger immediate alerts.
  + Regulatory Reporting: It can create and send automated reports while monitoring for compliance breaches.
  + Payment Routing: It evaluates real-time transaction fees, latency metrics, and regional authorization rates to route payments through the most cost-effective processor without human intervention.
  + Legacy System Migration: It can analyze, document, and rewrite outdated code, as shown in case studies where AI enhanced software development productivity by 30%.
  + Back-Office Automation: It handles data validation and document classification, freeing up thousands of hours of manual work.
  + Real-time Fraud Prevention: It monitors transactions continuously to identify anomalies, such as unusual logins or swift transfers, and can immediately freeze accounts or initiate multi-factor verification.
  + Dynamic Underwriting: Instead of relying on static reports, it gathers data from credit bureaus, bank feeds, and public records to evaluate loan risks in real-time.
  + Claims Management: In insurance, it handles information gathering and policy checks, improving claims accuracy.
  + Personal Finance Agents: It can act on behalf of users, such as finding the best mortgage rate, comparing products, and submitting applications.
  + Autonomous Investing: It can analyze real-time market data to rebalance portfolios and execute trades autonomously based on personalized risk profiles.
  + Proactive Advice: It can analyze spending habits to offer personalized budgeting advice and autonomously transfer funds to savings to avoid overdraft fees.

Despite its successes, it can bring risks such as AI hallucinations (inaccurate outputs), data security vulnerabilities, profiling, and model drift; where an agent's behavior changes unintentionally. These are violations and can have strict penalties, hence the assessment to follow.

As a concept, In a FinTech environment, where agentic AI moves beyond "chatbots" to autonomous actors (e.g., originating loans or filing regulatory reports), executive oversight during the concept phase is a collective responsibility led by the CEO and Board of Directors. They set the strategic vision and risk appetite before technical frameworks like RACI charts are formalized.Key executives who must oversee the initiative at the conceptual stage include:

  + Chief AI Officer (CAIO) / Head of AI: This emerging role is the primary bridge between technical potential and business impact. They own the AI agenda, including strategy, initial risk assessment, and value creation.
  + Chief Technology Officer (CTO): Responsible for the architectural blueprint of the "Agentic Enterprise". They evaluate whether the existing infrastructure can support the multi-layered orchestration required for autonomous agents.
  + Chief Information Officer (CIO): Focuses on how AI agents will integrate into existing ERP workflows and the "second-order thinking" required for long-term business transformation.
  + Chief Risk Officer (CRO): Oversees the "true risk posture" of the concept. In finance, this includes assessing "unknown unknowns" and how autonomous decisions might lead to market volatility or systemic risk.
  + General Counsel / Chief Legal Officer: Critical for assessing legal risks and ensuring the concept aligns with evolving regulations like the EU AI Act or SEC Marketing Rules.
  + Chief Compliance Officer (CCO): Ensures the conceptual design follows "Big G" (enterprise-wide) governance guardrails. They must verify that agents can operate within an "audit-ready" supervision regime.
  + Chief Financial Officer (CFO): Manages the financial implications, including the shift from CAPEX to OPEX as AI moves costs from payroll to software subscriptions.
  + Business Unit Heads (Owners): Since agentic AI often automates specific workflows (e.g., credit underwriting), the relevant business leader must be the "AI Owner" who stands behind the agent’s actions and results.

The group of executives approve the concept, then typically form a Responsible AI Governance Committee (or AI Steering Committee). This group is responsible for signing an AI Governance Committee Charter, which defines:

  + The Accountable Executive: Who falls legally acountable or loses their bonus if the agent makes a multi-million dollar error.
  + The Risk Tier: Whether the AI is High Risk (e.g., credit scoring) or Low Risk (e.g., internal document search), which dictates how many Consulted and Informed stakeholders are needed in the RACI Chart to be made.

Once the AI Governance Committee Charter is signed, the "concept" phase officially ends and the operationalization phase begins. The AI GRC Architect uses this signed mandate to move from high-level vision to enforceable technical and operational controls.
